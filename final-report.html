THE STORY

What happened, plainly

Release 4.2 shipped on 12 August — a long-requested, genuinely good change that weighted proximity higher so responders in wide or remote areas stopped losing jobs to someone with a better acceptance record 40 minutes away. It shipped alongside a smaller change: the answer window on a ping got cut from 90 seconds to 60. Acceptance rate dropped and complaints went up. The outgoing PM's read, in the handoff: probably seasonal, August is always soft. It wasn't. Four responders — Farlight, The Undertow, Vesper, and Meteor Mite — nearly stopped getting called at all, and kept getting worse, not better, for weeks. The cause wasn't the headline change. It was a quiet bug in how Rook scores a responder's recent reliability: a miss costs more than a take earns back, and nothing ever lets that score recover on its own. The shorter timeout just made misses more likely to happen in the first place.

MODULE 3 — REWIND

Ruling out "it's just seasonal"

"do you have access to last years data to rule out the seasonality impact?"— the PM's own prompt, 03-rewind/prompts.md
What it found: no prior-year data exists in the database, so the "it's seasonal" claim can't even be checked — and the acceptance-rate drop is a sharp cliff exactly at the 12 Aug release, not a gradual seasonal slide, which argues against seasonality anyway.
MODULE 4 — X-RAY VISION

Finding the actual bug in the code

"Find me the part of this code that takes points off somebody when they miss a ping or turn one down. Show it to me and explain it in plain English. Then find me every single thing in this code that puts points back on."— the PM's own prompt, 04-x-ray-vision/prompts.md
What it found: record_declined is the only place a responder's score ever drops; record_accepted is the only place it ever rises — anywhere in the whole codebase. No decay, no reset, no other path back.
MODULE 5 — SUPER SPEED

Watching it happen to one real responder

"Pick one responder from the database who went quiet. Show me every week for them, how many times we pinged them, how many they took. Then tell me what happened to that person, week by week, in plain English."— the PM's own prompt, 05-super-speed/prompts.md
What it found: Meteor Mite's share of their own local jobs collapsed from 52% to 16% after 4.2 — proof the company-wide average was quietly hiding a small group of people who never recovered.
MODULE 6 — SIDEKICKS

What nobody had said out loud yet

"Based on everything you now know about Rook, what should I be worried about that nobody has told me?"— the PM's own prompt, 06-sidekicks/prompts.md
What it found: the fix and the harm may share a population — 4.2's proximity change was built to help remote/wide-geography responders, but if that same group has less reliable signal, the shorter ping window may be hitting exactly them hardest.
THE MECHANISM

Why a quiet patch never got better on its own

Three numbers explain the whole trap.

−0.12
points lost for one missed or declined ping
+0.08
points gained for one taken ping — less than a miss costs
0
points gained for simply waiting — nothing ever drifts back on its own

A responder who misses a stretch of pings right after the window got shorter falls into a hole that's deeper to climb out of than it was to fall into — and the only way out is getting offered another job, which is exactly what a low score makes less likely. It's a trap with no floor and no expiry, flagged in the code itself as an open question since 2019 and never resolved.

THE HABIT

How this stops happening again

The fix solves this one bug. These solve the pattern — catching a half-built brief before it ships, not after.

SKILL

review-checklist

Checks any brief for four things that are easy to skip under deadline: a named owner, a real success measure, consistent scope start to finish, and the problem explained before the fix. Read-only — it reports, it doesn't rewrite.

SKILL

multi-model-review

Runs a document past a specific stakeholder — in character, across several different models in parallel — and reconciles the results into one agreement/discrepancy table instead of a single opinion. Used on this exact brief, by two different reviewers, and caught real issues each time.

ROUTINE

Weekly review-checklist

A standing habit, not a one-off: every Monday morning, the checklist runs automatically against whatever brief is current and reports what it finds — so the next one gets checked before it ships, not after.
